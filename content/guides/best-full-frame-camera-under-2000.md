Full frame is no longer a luxury. Price cuts on older bodies and a wave of cheaper new ones mean there are now more full-frame cameras under $2,000 than there have ever been. The question is which one is worth your money.

## Every full-frame camera under $2,000 today

This list builds itself. It takes every full-frame camera released since 2020 whose cheapest new, body-only price at a US store we check is under $2,000 right now, cheapest first. When prices move, the list moves with them.

## What you give up at this price

The cheaper bodies save money in a few predictable places. Look for these on each camera's page:

- **In-body stabilisation (IBIS).** The original [Canon EOS R8](/cameras/canon-eos-r8) doesn't have it, which matters with older or manual lenses. The [Nikon Z5 II](/cameras/nikon-z5-ii), [Nikon Zf](/cameras/nikon-zf) and [Sony A7 IV](/cameras/sony-a7-iv) do.
- **Viewfinder.** The [Panasonic Lumix S9](/cameras/panasonic-lumix-s9) and [Nikon Z5IIc](/cameras/nikon-z5iic) have none, which keeps them small. If you shoot in bright sun, you'll miss it.
- **Weather sealing.** Check the spec sheet before you take any of these out in the rain.
- **Card slots and battery life.** Cheaper bodies often have one slot and a smaller battery. That is fine for most people, but wedding and event shooters want two.

## Our picks

[[picks:nikon-z5-ii,canon-eos-r8,sony-a7-iv,nikon-zf]]

- **Best overall: Nikon Z5 II.** Stabilised, weather sealed and fast to focus, for less than many APS-C cameras.
- **Lightest: Canon EOS R8.** The smallest way into Canon's RF system, if you can live without IBIS.
- **Best for Sony lenses: Sony A7 IV.** The previous generation, now cheaper since the A7 V arrived, and still one of the best all-rounders.
- **Best looking: Nikon Zf.** Retro dials outside, the same core as Nikon's mid-range bodies inside.
