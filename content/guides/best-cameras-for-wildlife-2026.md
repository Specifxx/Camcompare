Wildlife photography asks three things of a camera: autofocus that finds an eye on a bird in a branch, a fast burst so you catch the wing up, and reach. Reach is where most of the money goes, and it is why a smaller sensor is often the smarter pick here: the crop makes every lens behave like a longer one.

## Crop or full frame?

A crop sensor gives you reach for free; full frame gives you cleaner pictures when the light goes, which for wildlife is the first and last hour of the day. If you mostly shoot small birds in daylight, crop wins. If you shoot mammals at dawn, full frame does. Our [full frame vs APS-C](/guides/full-frame-vs-aps-c) guide explains the trade-off, and [low-light performance explained](/guides/low-light-performance-explained) covers the light side.
