A thousand dollars buys more camera than it has at any point in the last decade. The kit that cost $1,500 three years ago is now under $1,000 because the makers have moved their flagships up and let last year's technology trickle down, and because stores discount a camera as soon as its successor is announced. This guide is every camera we track that a US store sells new for under $1,000 this morning, sorted by what it is for. Prices are live: they are read from the stores at every price check, so the figures below are today's, and the links go to the full comparison for each camera.

Two rules we applied. The price is the cheapest **new** price at a store we compare, body only or with the kit lens as the store lists it; used cameras are a separate question, covered in [used vs new camera gear](/guides/used-vs-new-camera-gear). And a camera only makes the list if more than one store sells it, so the price is a real market price and not one shop's clearance.

## Our picks

[[picks:canon-eos-r50,nikon-z50-ii,sony-zv-e10-ii,fujifilm-x-m5,canon-powershot-v1,dji-osmo-pocket-3]]

- **Best all-round camera under $1,000: Nikon Z50 II.** A 20.9 MP APS-C sensor, Nikon's newest autofocus (the subject detection from the Z8 and Z9) and a proper viewfinder, at {{price:nikon-z50-ii}} today. It is the camera to buy if you want one body that does stills and video well and a lens system to grow into.
- **Best value: Canon EOS R50.** Canon's small APS-C body is the cheapest way into a current mirrorless system with good autofocus, at {{price:canon-eos-r50}}. The RF-S lens range is still short, but the kit lens is fine and the body is tiny.
- **Best for video: Sony ZV-E10 II.** Built for creators: a 26 MP APS-C sensor, 4K 60p, a flip screen, no viewfinder. {{price:sony-zv-e10-ii}} today, and the E-mount has more lenses under $500 than any other.
- **Best for photographers who care about the look: Fujifilm X-M5.** The smallest X-series body, Fujifilm's film simulations, 6K open-gate video, no viewfinder. {{price:fujifilm-x-m5}} today, and often the hardest of the five to find in stock.
- **Best fixed-lens compact: Canon PowerShot V1.** A 1.4-inch sensor behind a 16–50mm-equivalent zoom, a cooling fan for long video, and a flip-out screen: {{price:canon-powershot-v1}}. The compact we would buy for travel.
- **Best pocket video camera: DJI Osmo Pocket 3.** A 1-inch sensor on a three-axis gimbal, {{price:dji-osmo-pocket-3}} today. It is not an interchangeable-lens camera, but for walking-and-talking video nothing else under $1,000 is as steady.

## What the extra money buys above $1,000

Spending more mostly buys three things: a full-frame sensor (cleaner high-ISO pictures and shallower depth of field), a better viewfinder and body, and faster burst shooting with the newest autofocus. If any of those is the reason you are buying a camera, the [best full-frame camera under $2,000](/guides/best-full-frame-camera-under-2000) is the next guide to read. If none of them is, the cameras above will outlast your interest in the hobby, and the money is better spent on [a second lens](/guides/first-lens-upgrade).

## Before you buy

- **Check the kit.** Stores list the same camera body-only and with a kit lens under different prices; the comparison page shows each listing's title so you can see which you are getting.
- **Add shipping and tax.** Our US prices exclude sales tax, which is added at checkout; the [grey market guide](/guides/grey-market-cameras) explains why an import can look cheaper than it is.
- **Set an alert.** Each camera page has an "Email me when the price drops" form; a camera a year old is usually discounted when its successor is announced.
